Building systems of belonging for people, institutions, and communities in transition.

I am a rabbi and a program operations leader. Most of my work comes back to one plain question: what makes a person stay visible to a community after they have already arrived?

What I work on

01

Belonging is designed

Isolation is not a personal failure. It is usually a design failure, and a design can be changed. Rooms, schedules, handoffs, and invitations quietly decide who stays visible and who slips out of view.

02

Counting is not knowing

A better participation number is not proof of impact. It is a more honest place to start. I am interested in measures that show two things at once: how deeply a mission reaches, and how broad the room actually is.

03

Relationships with no end date

Care that stops when a care level changes was never really care. The work is to follow the person: from home, to assisted living, to skilled nursing, without asking them to start over each time.

Who I am

I direct K'vod Connect, an initiative of Jewish Family Service of Cincinnati that works to end social isolation among older adults. I have led it since its 2020 launch, alongside a multidisciplinary team working across community programming, spiritual care, volunteer engagement, and evaluation.

In 2025, K'vod Connect received the Outstanding Project in the Field of Aging award from the Tristate Association for Professionals in Aging.

Aging is where I am doing this work right now. It is not the edge of the question. The same design problems show up anywhere people are moving between institutions, and anywhere a community assumes belonging will take care of itself.
